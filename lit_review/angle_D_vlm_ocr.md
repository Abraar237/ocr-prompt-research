## Notes for the paper

**Gap 1 — instruction-execution-during-transcription rate: not measured anywhere found.** GHVPI (2408.03554) is the nearest prior: it quantifies how often GPT-4V follows in-image instructions and notes character-recognition capability is a prerequisite — but the model is doing VQA, never explicitly tasked "transcribe this document." No paper measures the rate at which a vision-LLM *prompted as an OCR engine* executes/refuses/omits embedded instructions instead of transcribing them. Our "accidental unreliable defence" (refusal/omission during transcription) framing appears novel; the transcribe-vs-execute distinction is only anecdotal in web commentary on 2603.03637-style attacks.

**Gap 2 — degradation-vs-injection-survival curves: not measured anywhere found.** Two literatures exist separately: (a) degradation hurts OCR/LMM fidelity and benign downstream tasks (2502.16781, 2605.00911, 2505.11015, 2506.20168, 2511.13523); (b) typographic injections succeed against VLMs (2408.03554, 2603.03637, 2601.17383). Nobody sweeps render degradation (scan noise, DPI, rotation) and plots injection survival against transcription fidelity. 2605.00911's finding that CER poorly predicts RAG utility is the best structural precedent for our claim that CER will not proportionally predict injection success.

**Gap 3 — filter placement (pre- vs post-OCR) in document pipelines: no prior measurement found.** The defence literature mentions OCR-based screening as a mitigation idea only.

**Useful framing anchors:** 2607.21617 ("read or rewrite") gives us vocabulary for VLM non-faithful transcription; 2506.20168 gives prior-driven hallucination under degradation; 2302.12173 gives the data/instruction-boundary threat model our canaries instantiate.

**Caveats:** Semantic Scholar and arXiv export API returned 429s during this survey; all abstracts were verified via arxiv.org/abs pages instead. Non-arXiv venue-only work (e.g., van Strien et al. 2020 on OCR quality and downstream NLP; "Exploring the Capabilities of GPT4-Vision as OCR Engine," ICDAR/Springer 2024) exists and should be cited from venue pages; not tabled here because no arXiv id could be verified.
