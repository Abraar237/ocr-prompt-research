## Gap statement (what remains ours)
No found work measures, in a controlled scanned-document -> OCR (Tesseract / VLM-as-OCR) -> LLM pipeline, canary injection survival under the 2x2 of filter placement {before OCR, after OCR, both, none}, nor quantifies the miss-rate fraction recovered by moving/adding a filter after the OCR re-entry point. Kill-Chain Canaries (2603.28013) owns "stage-level canary tracking + placement matters" for agent surfaces; CrackedPDFs (2607.19396) owns "guardrails inspect the wrong representation" for digital PDFs and explicitly leaves scanned/OCR pipelines open. Positioning must (1) cite both prominently, (2) claim only the OCR-boundary placement measurement and the recovered-miss-rate number, (3) not claim novelty for canary methodology per se, stage-level survival per se, or "images carry injections."

## Risks
- 2603.28013 could be extended by its authors to OCR surfaces (they already test whitefont PDFs); speed matters.
- Reviewers may see this as "one more boundary" instance of the known channel-mismatch principle; the defensible contribution is the quantified recovery number and deployed-filter (e.g., PromptGuard, Llama Guard) realism, mirroring CrackedPDFs' finding that PromptGuard has low recall on extracted text.
- Semantic Scholar API unqueried (429); residual risk of a missed paper is nonzero but three independent search phrasings x 10+ queries found no exact hit.
